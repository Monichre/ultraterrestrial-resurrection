VERIFIED EVENT DOCUMENTATION & ANALYSIS METHODOLOGY
Comprehensive Guide for Fact-Checking and Cross-Reference Analysis

1. PRIMARY SOURCE VERIFICATION
1.1 Government Documents
- Official government records and reports
- Declassified intelligence documents
- Court transcripts and legal proceedings
- FOIA (Freedom of Information Act) release documents
- Certified copies maintained by government archives

1.2 Official Institutional Records
- Corporate annual reports and SEC filings
- University research documentation
- Hospital and medical records (when publicly available)
- Police and law enforcement reports
- Regulatory agency findings and citations

1.3 Direct Testimony & Documentation
- Eyewitness accounts with corroboration
- Participant diaries and letters
- Audio/video recordings with chain of custody
- News organization raw footage
- Social media posts with metadata verification

2. SECONDARY SOURCE CROSS-REFERENCING
2.1 Established Media Outlets
- Major newspapers with fact-checking departments (NYT, WSJ, WP, Guardian)
- Wire services (AP, Reuters, Bloomberg)
- Investigative journalism organizations (ProPublica, ICIR)
- Academic journalism reviews

2.2 Expert Analysis Sources
- Peer-reviewed academic papers
- Think tank reports (with bias disclosure)
- Subject matter expert publications
- Historical society documentation
- Professional association statements

2.3 Archival Sources
- Library of Congress records
- National Archives documents
- Presidential library collections
- University special collections
- Museum artifact documentation

3. FACT-CHECKING PROTOCOL
3.1 Source Hierarchy
Tier 1: Primary government documents, court records
Tier 2: Major news organizations with editorial oversight
Tier 3: Academic publications with peer review
Tier 4: Eyewitness accounts with corroboration
Tier 5: Secondary analysis and commentary

3.2 Cross-Reference Requirements
Minimum 3 independent sources for basic fact verification
5+ sources for controversial claims
10+ sources including primary documents for investigative claims
Expert validation for technical/ specialized claims

3.3 Bias Detection Methods
Source funding and affiliation analysis
Historical accuracy track record review
Editorial standards verification
Conflict of interest disclosure review
Multiple viewpoint documentation

4. ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORKS
4.1 Timeline Construction
Chronological event mapping
Preceding factors analysis
Sequential cause-effect relationships
Simultaneous events and context
Post-event consequences tracking

4.2 Stakeholder Analysis
Primary actors and participants
Secondary affected parties
Influential observers and commentators
Institutional roles and responsibilities
Power dynamics and relationships

4.3 Impact Assessment
Immediate consequences
Short-term effects (1-12 months)
Medium-term impacts (1-5 years)
Long-term historical significance
unintended consequences

5. DOCUMENTATION STANDARDS
5.1 Citation Requirements
Full source identification
Publication date and location
Archive reference numbers
Author credentials
Access verification dates

5.2 Evidence Chain Procedures
Digital forensics verification
Metadata preservation
Chain of custody documentation
Original context maintenance
Multiple format preservation

5.3 Controversy Documentation
Competing claims presentation
Source bias identification
Unverified allegations notation
Expert disagreement documentation
Ongoing investigation status

6. RED FLAGS FOR SOURCE RELIABILITY
Anonymous sources without corroboration
Single-source claims presented as fact
Conflicting eyewitness accounts without resolution
Government or institutional denials without evidence
Edited or incomplete document presentation
Unnamed officials or spokespersons
