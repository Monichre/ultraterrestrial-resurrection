# Design Analysis — Spy Lab War-Room Moodboard

> Vision-written gold pass. Date: 2026-08-13. Emphasis: mood / art direction.

## Source

- **Source type**: local image · **Path**: `extractions/spy-lab-war-room-moodboard/spy-lab-war-room-moodboard.png` (928×1232)
- **Capture method**: direct vision
- **Detected limitations**: collage seams and individual photo captions are below legibility; read as prop families, not individual frames.

## TL;DR

A war-room catalogued like a museum: monitors, a wall-sized watch photograph, terrain table, archive shelves, hanging photo strips. The spy-lab cluster's set-design bible in warm archival gray.

## 1. Visual identity

**Personality**: obsessive, methodical, analog, lived-in.
**Mood**: total documentation as comfort and mania.
**Detectable stylistic references**: 70s–80s intelligence-film production design, horological workshop aesthetics, museum-archive displays.
**Information density**: saturated — every panel is cluttered, but the grid imposes order.
**Implicit positioning**: worldbuilding reference; the room as a character.
**Confidence**: ✅ high.

### 1.2 Brand voice

Obsession made habitable. The design believes the audience reads care as credibility — that a room where every photograph is hung and every box is labeled tells you the work matters, and that analog clutter (paper, brass, CRT glass) signals a pre-digital patience.

### 1.3 The ONE brand thing

- **The thing**: the wall-sized black-and-white watch blow-up — horology elevated from prop to altar.
- **Why it carries the brand**: it fuses the two themes (time, surveillance) into one monumental image; remove it and the room is generic archive.
- **How everything else supports it**: monitors, terrain table, and photo strips stay small and subordinate; only the watch is allowed to be huge.
- **Where it appears**: the anchor panel; everywhere else it's deliberately absent. ⚠️ medium (usage inferred).

## 3. Components inventory

Signature: **watch-altar wall** + **hanging photo strips** + **terrain planning table**. Generic: none in UI terms — this is a photographed space, not an interface.

## 4. Layout & composition

Portrait plate, loose collage grid; anchor image top; survey reading order monitors → watch → table → desk; consistent warm-gray grade unifies disparate shots.

## 5. Reconstruction notes

As UI: asset-class worldbuilding plate. Quick wins: the warm-gray palette translates directly to a page theme (bone ground, bronze accent). Tricky bits: the collage's unity is *grading*, not layout — all panels share one tonal curve; don't mix color temperatures.

| Layer | Confidence | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | ✅ | moodboard read unambiguous |
| Colors | ✅ | pixel-grounded |
| Typography | ⚠️ | labels present, illegible |

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

**Do** — keep every surface in the bone/taupe family; use bronze sparingly for warmth; treat photos as documents (borders, tape, pins).

**Don't** — don't introduce saturated color; don't modernize the tech (no flatscreens); don't tidy the clutter away.
