## Ultraterrestrial Research Style Key

### Core Visual Themes

- **Cinematic Realism Meets Myth-Tech:** grounded textures and plausible tech with subtle sci-fi elements.
- **Disclosure-Era Aesthetic:** archival documents, old microphones, vintage gear mixed with advanced sensors.
- **Symbolic Resonance:** glyphs, constellations, ancient cartography, “god purse” iconography and sigils.
- **Field Research Grit:** dust, wind, mud, film grain and imperfect light.
- **Atmospheric Tension:** fog, shafts of light, lens flares and partial reveals.

### Color Palette

- **Base Earth Tones:** ochre, sand, deep brown and charcoal.
- **Accents:** muted teal, cold silver and soft amber glow.
- **Light Sources:** dim fluorescents, warm tungsten, pale moonlight or UFO radiance (cool white or faint violet).

### Lighting & Mood

- **Golden Hour Fieldwork:** warm low light and long shadows.
- **Cold Archive:** overhead fluorescents with a green or blue cast.
- **Crash Site Night Ops:** backlit fog, flashlight beams and silhouette focus.
- **Conference Room Shadows:** chiaroscuro lighting with visible dust motes.

### Composition

Subjects often sit slightly off-center (rule of thirds), giving space for implied off-frame presence. Use layered depth with foreground objects such as cameras, notes and debris. Let the background tell a story with hints of movement, data projections or distant lights.

### Texture & Detail

Weathered fabric, scratched lenses, smudged papers and grainy film. Surfaces include brushed metal, crumbling stone, desert grit and damp moss. Mix tactile physical props with faint holographic overlays.

### Keywords for Prompt Consistency

When prompting, sprinkle in keywords like: cinematic, highly detailed, photo-realistic, volumetric lighting, archival textures, investigative realism, disclosure-era aesthetics, mythic symbolism, field research grit, atmospheric tension, National Geographic meets 1970s Cold War sci-fi.

## 1. Core Visual Thesis

Ultraterrestrial should feel like the user has discovered a forbidden research archive that is still alive. The world is not futuristic; it is **post-disclosure archival**. Artifacts feel assembled by researchers, whistleblowers, intelligence analysts, occult historians and field investigators. The visual identity balances official documentation, analog degradation, anomalous photography, scientific/forensic notation and mythic-symbolic residue. **Evidence objects are the guiding principle.**

## 2. Aesthetic DNA

### Primary Keywords

Declassified dossier, anomalous evidence, black-budget archive, field investigation, occult science, myth-tech, forensic photography, UFO crash recovery, degraded scan, halftone surveillance image, classified document layout, analog photocopy, burned paper, redacted memo, government form, typewritten data, annotated margins, archival grid, military-industrial mysticism and disclosure-era research file.

### High-Level Mood

Credible, unsettling, academic, forbidden, weathered, procedural, mythic, damaged, intelligent and classified. Artifacts should feel like documents passed through too many hands, too many agencies and at least one dimensional event.

## 3. Design Principles

1. **Make everything feel found, not designed.** The viewer should feel like they found the artifact in a box labeled “PROPERTY OF SOMEONE WHO KNEW TOO MUCH.” Avoid overly clean poster design symmetry unless you deliberately contrast it with messy archival layers.
2. **The subject is evidence, not decoration.** UFOs, fires, landscapes, forests, debris, portraits, maps and symbols should feel like they were captured, cataloged, indexed and partially suppressed.
3. **Imperfection is the premium finish.** Paper folds, scan streaks, photocopy noise, halftone dots, torn edges, red pencil marks, misaligned grids, dust, burns and compression artifacts are part of the story.
4. **Typography should feel bureaucratic and unstable.** Use clean official typography mixed with broken typewriter text, corrupted labels, partial codes, misprinted headers, vertical marginalia and stamped identifiers.
5. **The app should be legible; the world should be mysterious.** Build a functional UI layer that is clean and structured and an artifact layer that is degraded and atmospheric. This keeps the product usable while still feeling Ultraterrestrial.

## 8. Typography Direction

### For Graphic Artifacts

Use corrupted government sans-serif, condensed grotesque headers, typewriter metadata, monospaced catalog text, microfilm-style labels, vertical marginal text, stamped uppercase and black-bar redactions.

### For Actual App UI

Do not make the whole app look like unreadable classified paperwork. Use clean sans-serif for navigation and body, monospace for metadata, condensed uppercase display type for section headers and keep text off the chaotic textures.

### Typography Roles

- **Display Header:** Big, official and slightly intimidating (e.g., “INCIDENT FILE”).
- **Metadata Text:** Small, monospaced and technical (e.g., “CASE ID: UT-0422-A”).
- **Annotation Text:** Handwritten, broken and marginal, used sparingly for notes and comments.

## 9. Layout System

### Document Layout Rules

Your strongest layouts use these moves:

1. **Large Header Block:** A bold title at top left creates official weight (e.g., “INCIDENT RECOVERY FORM 12B”).
2. **Evidence Window:** A large photographic block embedded in the page.
3. **Side Metadata Rail:** A thin column containing tables, symbols, numbers and stamps.
4. **Bottom Microtext:** Dense pseudo-legal/scientific text block across the bottom.
5. **Fold Seam:** A vertical or horizontal crease gives physicality.
6. **Misalignment:** Photos, lines and annotations should be slightly imperfect.
7. **Sparse + Dense Contrast:** Allow some areas to breathe while others become overloaded with codes and marks. Contrast is crucial; if everything is noisy, nothing feels classified.

## 14. App UI Design Library

The app should feel like a modern intelligence analysis tool built on top of a haunted archive. Use this system to avoid overwhelming the user while maintaining the brand atmosphere.

### UI Philosophy

The app has two layers: a **functional UI layer** that is clean, readable and structured, and an **artifact layer** that is degraded, mysterious and archival. Use the artifact layer as atmosphere, hierarchy and storytelling, not as the main interface.

### Main UI Modes

#### 1. Archive Mode

For browsing files, articles, research entries and historical cases.

- Aged document backgrounds.
- Case cards, folder tabs and metadata chips.
- Redacted preview blocks.

#### 2. Analysis Mode

For timelines, maps, relationship graphs, sightings and patterns.

- Dark grid interface.
- Thin technical lines.
- Coordinate systems and monochrome charts.
- Signal overlays and subdued green/blue-black panels.

#### 3. Field Mode

For user-submitted sightings, photos, notes, geolocation and incident capture.

- Mobile evidence forms.
- Camera/photo cards.
- Timestamp labels and location coordinates.
- Witness confidence sliders and upload status.

#### 4. Myth-Tech Mode

For symbolic, archaeological, religious, interdimensional and consciousness-related material.

- Pale archival pages.
- Glyph diagrams and circular overlays.
- Constellation maps and layered annotations.

### Dossier Header

Top of article/case pages.

- Large case title and file ID.
- Classification and source reliability.
- Last updated and category.
- Redaction accent.

**Example:**

```text
CASE FILE: SOCORRO LANDING
FILE ID: UT-NM-1964-0424
CLASSIFICATION: HISTORICAL / MULTI-SOURCE
STATUS: ACTIVE RESEARCH
```

### Metadata Rail

Side panel or collapsible drawer.

- Date, location and coordinates.
- Phenomenon type and witnesses.
- Evidence type and source quality.
- Related cases and symbolic overlap.

### Redaction Tag

Visual emphasis or status label.

Examples: `REDACTED`, `SEALED`, `PARTIAL`, `UNVERIFIED`, `LEAKED`, `ARCHIVAL`, `CONTESTED`, `ACTIVE`.

### Source Reliability Meter

Evaluate evidence quality without overclaiming.

- Anecdotal
- Single witness
- Multi-witness
- Documentary
- Correlated
- Chain-of-custody

Represent with a horizontal bar of five ticks (filled squares for reliability level).

### Anomaly Timeline

Show events over time.

- Dark grid or aged paper.
- Dots and vertical markers.
- Source labels and redacted gaps.
- “Missing interval” markers and hover reveals.

### Artifact Viewer

Display images, documents and diagrams.

- Zoomable image.
- Metadata overlay and annotation layers.
- Source notes and related symbols.
- Contrast/scan view toggles.

## 16. Motion & Interaction Library

### Motion Principles

Motion should feel analog, not slick. Avoid bubbly SaaS animation. Use scanline reveals, redaction wipes, paper slide transitions, document stack movement, flickering projector light, timestamp ticking, subtle image jitter, map ping pulses, dossier unfolding and glitch only when justified.

### Motion Tokens

- **SCAN_REVEAL:** A horizontal scan passes over an image, revealing metadata.
- **REDACTION_WIPE:** A black bar slides across text, then partially reveals it.
- **PAPER_SHIFT:** Cards move like physical documents on a desk.
- **SIGNAL_PULSE:** Subtle circular pulse around a map coordinate or anomaly.
- **ARCHIVE_FLICKER:** A faint brightness fluctuation like a projector or old scanner.

## 37. Logo / Wordmark Direction

## 38. Brand Voice

### Voice Traits

Investigative, precise, skeptical but open, mythically aware, calm under pressure. Avoid sensationalism, debunker smugness and conspiracy-chaos. Treat anomalies seriously — neither mock nor hype.

### Good Voice

> “This report contains three independent witness claims, one disputed photographic artifact and one symbolic parallel from earlier historical material.”

### Bad Voice

> “ALIENS CONFIRMED?? THE GOVERNMENT HID THIS FROM YOU!!!”

### Sentence Pattern

Use: `[Evidence] suggests [possibility], but [limitation] remains unresolved.`

Example: The witness consistency suggests a shared observation event, but the lack of sensor data keeps the case unresolved. This tone builds trust.

## 54. Signature Product Ideas

1. **Anomalous Residue Score** — After conventional explanations are evaluated, show what remains unexplained.
2. **Source Chain Viewer** — Visualize how a claim moves through witnesses, books, documents, podcasts, articles and official records.
3. **Mythic Pattern Matcher** — Connect modern reports to recurring ancient motifs without claiming direct proof.
4. **Case Confidence Ledger** — Track why a case moves from “unverified” to “contested” to “multi-source” and beyond.
5. **Field Report Intake** — Let users submit sightings in a structured, serious format.
6. **Evidence Layer Toggle** — Allow users to switch between original, enhanced, annotated and source context views.
7. **Redacted Mode** — Provide a setting that hides unverified claims or low-confidence material, empowering users to choose epistemic strictness.
