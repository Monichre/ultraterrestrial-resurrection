# Ultraterrestrial Research — Master Brand Bible

## Visual Identity Statement

Ultraterrestrial is a living archive of anomalous intelligence, combining declassified documentation, forensic field research, mythological pattern recognition, and disclosure-era analysis. Its visual language is damaged, procedural, symbolic, and unsettlingly credible — a research system for impossible events treated with serious investigative discipline.

## Core Rule

> Does this make the impossible feel investigable?

This is the deciding rule for every visual, product, editorial, and interaction decision.

## Final Direction

The strongest expression of the project says:

> “This is not science fiction. This is the paperwork left behind after reality got breached.”

## Core Visual Thesis

Ultraterrestrial should feel like the user has discovered a forbidden research archive that is still alive. The world is not futuristic; it is post-disclosure archival. Artifacts feel assembled by researchers, whistleblowers, intelligence analysts, occult historians, and field investigators.

The system balances:

* Official documentation.
* Analog degradation.
* Anomalous photography.
* Scientific and forensic notation.
* Mythic-symbolic residue.
* Modern analytical overlays, used sparingly.

Evidence objects are the guiding principle.

## Aesthetic DNA

### Primary Keywords

Declassified dossier, anomalous evidence, black-budget archive, field investigation, occult science, myth-tech, forensic photography, UFO crash recovery, degraded scan, halftone surveillance image, classified document layout, analog photocopy, burned paper, redacted memo, government form, typewritten data, annotated margins, archival grid, military-industrial mysticism, disclosure-era research file.

### Mood

Credible, unsettling, academic, forbidden, weathered, procedural, mythic, damaged, intelligent, classified.

Artifacts should feel like documents passed through too many hands, too many agencies, and at least one dimensional event.

## Design Principles

1. **Make everything feel found, not designed.** The viewer should feel they discovered the artifact in a box labeled “PROPERTY OF SOMEONE WHO KNEW TOO MUCH.”
2. **The subject is evidence, not decoration.** UFOs, fires, landscapes, forests, debris, portraits, maps, and symbols should feel captured, cataloged, indexed, and partially suppressed.
3. **Imperfection is the premium finish.** Folds, scan streaks, photocopy noise, halftone dots, torn edges, red pencil marks, misaligned grids, dust, burns, and compression artifacts are part of the story.
4. **Typography should feel bureaucratic and unstable.** Mix clean official typography with broken typewriter text, corrupted labels, partial codes, misprinted headers, vertical marginalia, and stamped identifiers.
5. **The app should be legible; the world should be mysterious.** Build a clean functional UI layer and a degraded atmospheric artifact layer.

## Texture Stack

1. Paper grain.
2. Dust specks.
3. Scanline streaks.
4. Fold seam.
5. Halftone dots.
6. Ink bleed.
7. Redaction blocks.
8. Handwritten annotations.
9. Marginal grid.
10. Compression / photocopy decay.

Recommended UI opacity:

* Paper grain: 5–12%.
* Scanlines: 3–8%.
* Halftone: 5–15%.
* Redaction: 85–100%.
* Fold seams: 8–20%.
* Handwriting: 20–60%.
* Background glyphs: 3–10%.

Never let texture compete with text.

## Document Layout Rules

* Central evidence window.
* Side metadata rail.
* Bottom microtext block.
* Fold seams.
* Slight misalignment.
* Sparse + dense contrast.

## Editorial Voice

Investigative, precise, skeptical but open, mythically aware, calm under pressure.

Avoid:

* Sensationalism.
* Debunker smugness.
* Conspiracy chaos.
* “Aliens confirmed” language.

Sentence pattern:

**[Evidence] suggests [possibility], but [limitation] remains unresolved.**

Example:

> The witness consistency suggests a shared observation event, but the lack of sensor data keeps the case unresolved.

## Quality Checklist

### Visual Fit

* Does the piece feel like evidence?
* Does it feel classified or merely decorative?
* Does it look grounded and plausible?
* Does it avoid generic sci-fi tropes?
* Does it have analog imperfection?
* Is there enough negative space?
* Is the typography believable?
* Does the artifact tell a story?

### UI Fit

* Is the UI readable?
* Is the hierarchy obvious?
* Are controls visually separate from texture?
* Does the mystery support the task?
* Is there too much noise?
* Does the screen still work without the aesthetic layer?

### Brand Fit

* Would this belong in a declassified anomalous archive?
* Would it embarrass us next to serious research?
* Does it feel open-minded without being gullible?
* Does it feel skeptical without being smug?

## Negative Prompt Library

Avoid:

* Clean vector art.
* Glossy 3D render.
* Cartoon aliens.
* Cute aliens.
* Neon cyberpunk overload.
* Colorful spaceships.
* Fantasy armor.
* Superhero poses.
* Polished sci-fi concept art.
* Generic NASA posters.
* Smooth plastic surfaces.
* Perfect typography.
* Sterile corporate UI.
* Anime, Pixar, Marvel, comic-book styles.
* Luxury tech branding.

Stay in the lane of damaged evidence, not space opera.
